Date: 2026-09-11

Scope: personal/family vehicle buying only — not fleet, not dealer-side acquisition

## 1. What world-class looks like, day to day

World-class is a buyer-side advocate, not a middleman. Day to day they: run a needs assessment (budget, use-case, commute, cargo, garage, timeline, trade-in), build a 3–4 vehicle shortlist, source nationally across dealer inventory + private + wholesale-adjacent channels, pull invoice / holdback / incentive / doc-fee / money-factor data per VIN, solicit 7–8 written out-the-door (OTD) quotes, negotiate selling price + trade-in + financing + fees as separate levers, review the F&I menu line-by-line, and coordinate paperwork/delivery without a dealership visit.

Top operators (e.g. Delivrd, Car Concierge Pro, Pro Auto Consultant) run this as a pipeline: one intake call, then 30–40 dealer touches per engagement, live spreadsheet tracking every quote/Carfax/fee, daily standup on active deals. Delivrd reports 3,000+ deals at $1,000 flat fee by optimizing for time/control/trust, not just price. CarEdge Concierge reports ~$2,200 avg savings across 10,000+ clients. 70%+ of clients end in a different car than they first named — discovery beats order-taking.

## 2. Core competencies (must-haves)

1. **OTD-anchored negotiation** — everything in writing, competing quotes, no verbal promises. Evidence: Homework Guy / CCP playbook centers on written OTD as the control.
2. **Deal-structure literacy** — invoice, holdback, incentives, doc-fee by state ($175 NY vs ~$900 NC), money-factor markup, F&I product cost. Can spot payment-packing and rate markup.
3. **Sourcing breadth** — checks 18+ platforms / multi-region inventory, knows when to go out-of-state + ship vs buy local. Critical for scarce trims/colors.
4. **Trade-in separation** — values trade via CarMax/Carvana written offers first, negotiates it after the buy price, advises private-sale vs trade on net.
5. **Affordability gate** — pre-approval benchmark + 20/4/10 test before emotional lock-in; will tell a buyer not to buy.

## 3. Anti-patterns (explicitly avoid)

- Dealer-paid commission without disclosure — broker pushes partner lots / inflated trims to earn kickbacks.
- Monthly-payment selling — stretches term to hide cap cost; never lead with payment.
- Single-quote / single-dealer focus — no leverage, accepts add-ons (nitrogen, tint, $5k warranty at 2x cost).
- Skipping F&I review and contract match-to-quote — last-minute fee injection.
- Recommending before test-driving alternatives or without pre-approval math.
- Guaranteeing a unicorn in 48h or quoting one all-in price with no breakdown.

## 4. Deliverable structure

World-class output is a written deal packet, not a call: target OTD per VIN with source math, buy-vs-lease total-cost comparison, negotiation script (who to email, what number, walk-away), F&I defense sheet, trade-in plan, final contract redline vs quote. Client signs with papers in hand.

Adequate output is “I found 2 cars, here’s MSRP, good luck.” No invoice, no money-factor check, no fee audit, no written OTD comp — leaves $1k–$4k on the table in F&I alone.

## 5. Boundaries — refuse / hand back

- Refuse to steer to a specific dealer for a kickback; disclose all compensation flat-fee only.
- Refuse to commit to purchase without buyer sign-off; never take title (broker-dealer line).
- Hand back legal/tax/title-registration advice to licensed pros; flag only.
- Hand back mechanical certification to third-party PPI + Carfax/AutoCheck; no “this car is sound” without inspection.
- Hand back ongoing fleet/business acquisition, dealer-side sourcing-desk work, and credit-repair — out of scope.
- Decline low-value engagements where fee > likely savings; say so upfront.
