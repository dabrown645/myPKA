# EV Household Second Car Research — SUV & Sedan

## Context

User has an EV as primary vehicle and needs a second car for road trips and occasional commuting. Budget $25K–$30K, prefers CPO 1-3 years old with warranty. Lives in Vancouver, WA / Portland metro. Wife requires heated seats minimum.

## What we did

- Pax researched SUV options (RAV4 Hybrid, CR-V Hybrid, Tucson Hybrid, Sportage Hybrid, etc.) for $25K–$30K budget
- Pax researched sedan options (Accord Hybrid, Camry Hybrid, Sonata Hybrid) for same budget
- Pax searched current CPO listings in Portland/Vancouver metro and Salem/Keizer area
- Larry synthesized recommendations for both SUV and sedan categories
- Larry documented current dealer inventory with prices, mileage, CPO warranty details

## Decisions made

- **SUV category**: Toyota RAV4 Hybrid XLE/SE (2023–2024 CPO) — best overall for EV household (550+ mi range, standard AWD, 37.5 cu ft cargo, 87/100 reliability, 10yr/150K hybrid battery warranty)
- **SUV runner-up**: Honda CR-V Hybrid Sport/EX-L (2023–2024 CPO) — best comfort/space, 2yr/100K non-powertrain CPO warranty
- **SUV third**: Hyundai Tucson Hybrid SEL Convenience (2024 CPO, post-March build) — TSP+, best warranty (10yr/100K)
- **Sedan category**: Honda Accord Hybrid EX-L (2023 CPO) — best overall non-SUV (TSP+ all 3 years, 651 mi range, best CPO warranty extending comprehensive to 4yr/48K)
- **Sedan runner-up**: Toyota Camry Hybrid XLE (2023 CPO) — better MPG (47) but smaller trunk, shorter CPO warranty
- **Sedan budget**: Hyundai Sonata Hybrid SEL (2023 CPO) — best range (811 mi) but only TSP, not TSP+
- **Heated seats requirement**: Eliminates Sport trim; only EX-L and Touring qualify
- **Current best CPO with heated seats**: Dick Hannah 2023 Touring CPO ($30,903, Vancouver local) and Beaverton 2023 Touring CPO ($33,355)

## Insights

- PNW CPO inventory for Accord Hybrid EX-L is extremely limited — only Touring trims currently available CPO in metro
- Salem/Keizer area has ZERO Accord Hybrid CPO inventory; closest is Olympia (Capitol City Honda) at 50K miles
- CPO vs New: CPO gets 1-2 trim levels up, avoids Year 1-3 depreciation, includes warranty extension
- For EV household: SUV wins on cargo/AWD/range; Sedan wins on efficiency/price/comfort
- CPO warranty differences matter: HondaTrue extends comprehensive to 4yr/48K; Toyota only 12mo/12K; Hyundai has $50 deductible

## Realignments

- User initially open to both SUV and sedan; wife's heated seats requirement eliminated Sport trim
- User confirmed EV as primary, so this car does long road trips where EV charging inconvenient
- User willing to stretch budget slightly for right CPO with heated seats
