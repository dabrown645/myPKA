# Teen Driver Car Shopping Research

## Context

The user came in asking for help with car shopping for a 16-year-old new driver in the Pacific Northwest (Portland/Vancouver OR/WA area). Initial requirements: small SUV, 1-5 years old, top IIHS safety ratings, adaptive cruise control, automatic emergency braking, ~40 MPG, and a $15K budget. The car needs to be reliable enough for the teen to take to college.

## What we did

- Pax conducted comprehensive research on small SUVs that meet the safety and feature requirements
- Pax provided detailed comparison of Hyundai Tucson Hybrid, Kia Sportage Hybrid, Toyota RAV4 Hybrid, and Honda CR-V Hybrid
- Pax delivered budget reality check: hybrid SUVs with all features start at $18K+, exceeding the $15K budget
- User clarified openness to sedans and hatchbacks, expanding the search
- Pax expanded research to include sedans and hatchbacks: Toyota Corolla Hybrid, Hyundai Elantra Hybrid, Kia Niro Hybrid, Toyota Prius, Mazda 3
- Pax researched Subaru models (Crosstrek, Impreza, Forester) to check if any meet criteria
- Larry consolidated all findings into a single summary document at `Deliverables/2026-08-24-teen-driver-car-shopping-summary.md`

## Decisions made

- **Question:** What vehicle type should be prioritized?
  **Decision:** Expand beyond SUVs to include sedans and hatchbacks, as they better fit the $15K budget while meeting safety and MPG targets.

- **Question:** Which vehicles are the top recommendations?
  **Decision:** Hyundai Elantra Hybrid SEL (2021) as budget winner ($13-15K), Toyota Corolla Hybrid LE (2021-2022) as best overall ($15-17K), Kia Niro Hybrid EX (2021) as SUV-like alternative.

- **Question:** Do any Subarus meet the criteria?
  **Decision:** No Subaru meets the 40 MPG target. Subaru Crosstrek Hybrid is closest (35 MPG) but falls short. Subaru options only viable if AWD is prioritized over MPG.

## Insights

- $15K budget is tight for 1-5 year old hybrid vehicles with top safety ratings and adaptive cruise control
- Hyundai/Kia 10-year/100K powertrain warranty is a significant advantage for a teen taking the car to college
- Toyota Corolla Hybrid offers the best reliability but sits at the edge of the budget
- PNW runs 5-10% above national average for used vehicles, especially Toyotas
- Insurance costs vary significantly by model - Mazda 3 and Toyota Prius are most expensive for teens

## Realignments
